    HOW IT WORKS

    The pipeline as it actually stands — architecture, augmentation, protocol, and the rules we held ourselves to.

    1 · The data

    60 brain MRI cases, four co-registered channels each (FLAIR, T1w, T1-Gd, T2w), skull-stripped, 240×240×155 at 1 mm isotropic resolution. An expert has drawn three tumour sub-labels — oedema, non-enhancing core, enhancing tumour — which we collapse to one binary target: label > 0, the BraTS "whole tumour" region. See F01 and F03 for what that looks like.

    2 · Preprocessing

    • Brain mask — any voxel non-zero in any channel. Background air is excluded from every statistic and every metric.
    • Per-case, per-channel z-score inside the brain mask — mandatory, not optional. Brain-mean FLAIR spans an 11.9× range across the cohort with no clinical meaning (F02); a model fed raw intensities learns which case it's looking at.
    • Crop to a fixed 192×192 in-plane box that clears every case's brain with margin, asserted against all 60 volumes, not sampled.

    3 · Data augmentation

    With 48 cases per training fold, augmentation is not polish — it is most of what stands between the model and memorising 48 brains. Ranges are deliberately mild: this is MRI, and a 45° rotation or a 2× intensity swing would manufacture anatomy that does not occur in vivo. Everything below is applied on the GPU, per sample, freshly randomised every time a patch is drawn.

    Geometry is one combined affine rather than three sequential resamples — each resample blurs, so doing it once costs one interpolation instead of three. The mask rides the same transform with nearest-neighbour sampling and is re-binarised afterwards, so it can never drift out of register with the image.

    4 · The model

    A 3D residual U-Net: 8,959,163 parameters, depth 4, base width 20, trained on random 96×160×160 voxel patches at batch size 8. All four modalities go in as channels; one binary tumour probability comes out. InstanceNorm3d throughout rather than batch norm — with 48 cases per fold and a 3D patch batch of 8, per-batch statistics are too noisy to normalise against.

    5 · Deep supervision

    The two ◎ aux heads above are 1×1×1 convolutions on intermediate decoder stages, each producing a coarse tumour map that is scored during training and discarded afterwards. They inject correction signal directly into the middle of the network instead of making it travel all the way back from the output. Weights halve with resolution and are normalised to sum to 1 — otherwise turning deep supervision on would also scale the total loss, and therefore the effective learning rate, making an architecture ablation secretly a learning-rate ablation.

    The lowest-resolution decoder stage gets no head: at 1/16 scale the smallest lesion in this cohort is a handful of voxels and the head mostly learns noise. Targets are matched to each head's resolution by average pooling, giving soft occupancy fractions. Average versus nearest-neighbour was measured, not argued — average won, 0.905 against 0.898 mean out-of-fold Dice.

    6 · Training protocol

    • Loss — soft Dice + BCE, equally weighted. Dice handles the class imbalance directly; BCE keeps early training stable while the network still predicts near-zero foreground everywhere.
    • Optimiser — AdamW, weight decay 1e-4, OneCycleLR stepped per batch with max_lr = 1e-3. 3e-3 was tried first and destabilised training under fp16: from the LR peak onward almost every batch produced a non-finite loss.
    • Mixed precision — fp16 autocast with gradient scaling, gradient-norm clipping at 1.0, and a non-finite-loss guard that skips and logs the step rather than letting one bad update poison every later forward pass.
    • 5-fold cross-validation — 48 train / 12 validation per fold, fold membership computed once from a fixed seed and shared by both baselines and the model, so every comparison is on identical held-out sets. Every case gets an out-of-fold prediction from a model that never saw it.
    • Checkpoint selection — best validation Dice, on a frozen validation patch set. It was not always frozen: patches were originally re-drawn every epoch, so "best epoch" chased a moving target and three of five folds locked in weights from before the learning-rate peak, un-annealed. Fixing that, plus gating early stopping to the second half of the schedule, is what took worst-case Dice from 0.139 to 0.612.

    7 · Inference

    • Sliding window over the full 155×192×192 crop — 12 overlapping patches per case at 50% stride, uniformly averaged. Coverage is asserted, not assumed: no voxel is left unpredicted.
    • Test-time augmentation — left–right flip, averaged with the unflipped prediction. Legitimate under strict out-of-fold evaluation because it reuses only the same single model that never saw the case.
    • Post-processing — threshold 0.5, keep the largest connected components above 500 voxels, and never predict outside the brain mask.
    • For a genuinely unseen scan, all five fold models vote and their probabilities are averaged. This is deliberately not used for the 60 development cases: four of the five trained on any given one of them, so an "ensemble" score there would be leaked rather than merely optimistic.

    9 · Ground rules

    1. Ask for the plan before writing files.
    2. Never accept a number you have not seen a figure for.
    3. Decide what a good result looks like before you produce one.
    4. Report what you found, not what you hoped for.

    10 · Limitations, stated up front

    • n = 60. Every metric will carry a confidence interval; a small cohort bounds what we're entitled to conclude.
    • Binary whole-lesion target only. Numbers here are not comparable to published tumour-core or enhancing-tumour scores.
    • Provenance withheld. The dataset's source and citation are held back by the course until the lab ends — nothing here should be compared to a named benchmark as if it were the same test set.
